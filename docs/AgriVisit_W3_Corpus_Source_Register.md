4. How exclusion works
Layer	Scope	Rule
1. Section	A whole section under one heading	A heading matching the excluded list — for example "how much pesticide" — removes the section until the next genuine multi-level section heading
2. Sentence	One sentence or bullet item	A quantity expressed per litre or in a stated number of litres is an application rate whatever the substance, so the sentence carrying it is removed and the guidance around it kept
3. Chunk	A whole passage	A backstop for amounts stated across several sentences: a chunk carrying both a chemical indicator and a measured volume is dropped entirely
Each layer exists because the one before it was shown to fail on a real document. Layer 1 depends on the publisher having used a heading, which one manual did not. Layer 2 matches the shape of the information rather than its vocabulary, so it does not degrade as the corpus grows.
5. Accepted cost
•	Fertiliser and lime mixing rates are removed alongside pesticide rates. Distinguishing them would require identifying what is being dissolved, which reintroduces a vocabulary list; application rates are not needed for a visit checklist.
•	One coffee passage on hardening off and acclimatisation is dropped, because a fertiliser volume and a passing mention of pesticides appear in the same chunk.
•	Eleven rows of an agro-chemical product table are removed: product strengths written as grams per litre have the shape of a mix ratio.
•	A total of 122 bulleted instruction lines in one manual are truncated by the PDF reader and are absent from the corpus. This is a limitation of extraction, not of exclusion, and is recorded as a Week 3 finding.
